A step that works 95% of the time sounds solid. Chain ten of them and it’s closer to a coin flip with good odds.

Illustrative math (assuming independent steps): 0.95 × 0.95 … ten times ≈ 0.60. Real systems differ, but the direction holds: long tasks amplify small error rates.

So evaluate the whole task, many times:
→ end-to-end success rate over dozens of runs
→ where in the chain failures cluster
→ which steps need a check or a human approval

(Numbers in the reel are illustrative, not measurements of a real system.)
